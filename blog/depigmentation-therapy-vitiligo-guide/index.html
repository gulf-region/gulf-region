What Is Depigmentation Therapy?

Depigmentation therapy is a dermatological treatment that removes remaining skin pigmentation from patients with extensive vitiligo, creating a uniform skin appearance by matching the white vitiligo patches rather than restoring colour to them. The primary agent used is monobenzone cream (monobenzyl ether of hydroquinone / MBEH), which is the only FDA-approved pharmaceutical for this purpose. The distinction matters because these agents are not interchangeable: monobenzone's permanent, irreversible effect makes it appropriate only for extensive vitiligo (50%+ body surface area) where a uniform depigmented result is the goal, while reversible options like hydroquinone or phototherapy remain first-line for limited vitiligo or non-vitiligo hyperpigmentation. A dermatologist can help determine which category fits a specific case before treatment begins.

This approach represents a philosophical shift from repigmentation: instead of fighting the vitiligo by attempting to restore colour, depigmentation therapy works with the vitiligo by completing the process of depigmentation uniformly across the body.

Who Is a Candidate for Depigmentation Therapy?

Depigmentation therapy is appropriate for a specific group of patients. Ideal candidates are:

  • Adults with vitiligo covering more than 50% of body surface area
  • Patients for whom repigmentation therapy has failed after 12+ months (steroids, NB-UVB, PUVA, tacrolimus, JAK inhibitors)
  • Patients with stable, non-progressing extensive vitiligo
  • Patients who have made a fully informed, considered, permanent decision to depigment
  • Patients willing to commit to lifelong SPF 50+ sunscreen after treatment
⚠️ Depigmentation therapy is not appropriate for patients with limited vitiligo, patients who have not tried repigmentation therapy, or anyone seeking cosmetic skin lightening.

How Monobenzone Depigmentation Works

Monobenzone (MBEH) is metabolised by tyrosinase inside melanocytes, generating quinone free radicals that permanently destroy the pigment-producing cells. This process is progressive — treated areas gradually lose their melanocytes over weeks and months of twice-daily application, producing permanent, stable depigmentation.

The standard starting concentration is 20% (equivalent to original Benoquin cream). Patients who have established tolerance may progress to 40% for faster results, or in some specialist-supervised cases to 60%.

The Treatment Protocol

  1. Consultation: Qualified dermatologist assessment of vitiligo extent, previous treatment history, and informed consent
  2. Starting concentration: Begin with Monobenzone 20% to assess skin tolerance over 4-8 weeks
  3. Application: Thin layer twice daily (morning and evening) to pigmented areas only
  4. Sun protection: SPF 50+ broad-spectrum sunscreen applied every morning from day one — mandatory
  5. Monitoring: Regular dermatologist assessment to evaluate progress and manage side effects
  6. Concentration adjustment: May progress to 40% after tolerance is established
  7. Completion: Treatment continues until complete depigmentation is achieved — typically 9-12 months

After Treatment — Life with Depigmented Skin

Depigmented skin has permanently lost its melanin UV protection. This requires lifelong adaptation:

  • Lifelong SPF 50+ sunscreen on all depigmented skin — every day, year-round
  • Protective clothing (long sleeves, hat) during outdoor activities
  • Avoiding peak UV hours (11am-3pm) when possible
  • For GCC patients: summer temperatures make strict sun protection especially important

Despite these requirements, many patients report significantly improved quality of life and self-confidence after successful depigmentation therapy.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if I am a candidate for depigmentation therapy?
Generally, depigmentation therapy is considered for adults with vitiligo covering more than 50% of body surface area, for whom repigmentation therapy has failed after 12+ months of treatment. A board-certified dermatologist can assess your suitability after examining your vitiligo extent and treatment history.
Is depigmentation therapy permanent?
Yes. Monobenzone permanently destroys melanocytes. The depigmentation does not reverse when treatment is stopped. This permanence is both the strength of the treatment (stable, long-term results) and a reason to make the decision carefully.
How long does depigmentation therapy take?
Complete treatment typically takes 9-12 months of twice-daily monobenzone application. Using 40% concentration instead of 20% can reduce this by 2-4 months. Individual results vary.
Is depigmentation therapy painful?
Depigmentation therapy is not painful. Initial skin redness and itching (common in the first few weeks) are the typical uncomfortable effects, but these are usually manageable and reduce over time.
What happens if I stop depigmentation therapy before completing treatment?
Stopping early may result in incomplete, patchy depigmentation — with some areas depigmented and others still pigmented. If you need to pause, consult your dermatologist first rather than stopping abruptly.
