About Monobenzone Cream — The Basics

1. What is monobenzone cream?
Monobenzone cream is a prescription topical pharmaceutical containing monobenzone (monobenzyl ether of hydroquinone, MBEH) as the active ingredient. It is FDA-approved for permanent depigmentation in patients with extensive vitiligo.

2. What is Monozan by Behvazan?
Monozan is the brand name for monobenzone 20% cream manufactured by Behvazan Pharmaceutical Co. in Rasht, Iran. It is the most widely distributed pharmaceutical-grade monobenzone cream in the GCC region and the recognized equivalent of the discontinued Benoquin cream.

3. Is monobenzone the same as Benoquin?
Yes. Both contain monobenzone (MBEH) at 20% concentration as the active ingredient. Benoquin was simply a brand name — the product has been discontinued. Monozan by Behvazan is the pharmaceutical-grade equivalent with identical active ingredient and therapeutic effect.

4. What is MBEH?
MBEH stands for monobenzyl ether of hydroquinone — the chemical name for monobenzone. It is a derivative of hydroquinone but significantly more potent, as it permanently destroys melanocytes rather than simply suppressing melanin production.

5. When was monobenzone approved by the FDA?
Monobenzone was approved by the FDA in 1952 (NDA 010596), making it one of the longest-standing dermatological pharmaceutical approvals in history.

6. What is monobenzone used for?
Monobenzone is approved for permanent depigmentation in patients with extensive vitiligo — specifically those with vitiligo covering 50% or more of body surface area. It is not indicated for cosmetic skin lightening.

7. Is monobenzone a bleaching cream?
No. Monobenzone is a depigmenting pharmaceutical, not a cosmetic bleaching cream. It permanently destroys melanocytes. Using it for general skin lightening without a vitiligo indication would be medically inappropriate.

8. What concentrations are available?
20% (30g tube, from $12), 40% (100g tube, $55), and 60% (100g tube, $75). All available from GulfDermaCare.

9. What is the difference between 20%, 40%, and 60%?
All three achieve the same permanent depigmentation result. The difference is speed: higher concentrations produce faster melanocyte destruction. 20% is the standard starting point. 40% works approximately 4-8 weeks faster. 60% is for advanced cases only under medical supervision.

10. Is monobenzone natural?
No. Monobenzone is a synthetic pharmaceutical compound. It is not derived from natural plant sources. It is manufactured to pharmaceutical standards.

Treatment and Application Questions

11. How do I apply monobenzone cream?
Apply a thin layer to pigmented skin areas only, twice daily (morning and evening). Wash hands immediately after. Apply SPF 50+ sunscreen to all treated areas. Apply to completely dry skin.

12. How long does monobenzone take to work?
Weeks 1-6: no visible change. Months 2-4: first lightening. Months 4-9: significant results. Months 9-12: complete depigmentation.

13. Can I apply monobenzone on my face?
Yes, with extra care. Use a very thin layer. Use 20% concentration only for facial areas. Avoid eyes, lips, and inside the nose. Follow dermatologist guidance.

14. How often should I apply monobenzone?
Twice daily — morning and evening, approximately 12 hours apart. Never more than twice daily.

15. Can I apply monobenzone to my entire body?
Treatment of large body surface areas is possible but increases side effect risk. Follow your dermatologist's guidance on treatment area extent.

16. What happens if I miss a dose?
Apply as soon as you remember, then return to your regular twice-daily schedule. Do not double-dose. Consistent application produces the best results.

17. Should I start with 20% or can I start with 40%?
Always start with 20% to assess skin tolerance, unless under direct dermatologist supervision who recommends otherwise. Progress to 40% after 4-8 weeks of good tolerance at 20%.

18. Can I stop treatment midway?
You can stop treatment at any time, but incomplete treatment may result in patchy, uneven depigmentation. Consult your dermatologist before stopping.

19. How long is a typical treatment course?
Most patients require 9-12 months for complete depigmentation. Using higher concentrations (40%, 60%) can reduce this timeline.

20. Does monobenzone work on all skin types?
Monobenzone is effective across all Fitzpatrick skin types (I-VI). Darker skin types may show faster initial results due to higher melanocyte density.

Safety and Side Effects Questions

21. Is monobenzone cream safe?
Monobenzone has been FDA-approved since 1952 with a well-established safety profile for its approved indication. The main consideration is its permanent effect and increased sun sensitivity after treatment.

22. What are the main side effects?
Common: skin redness and itching (30-50%, particularly in weeks 1-8). Universal: permanent sun sensitivity requiring lifelong SPF 50+. Rare: contact dermatitis, exogenous ochronosis.

23. Is monobenzone safe during pregnancy?
Not recommended during pregnancy or breastfeeding without explicit physician guidance. Avoid unless specifically advised by a qualified physician after benefit-risk assessment.

24. Can children use monobenzone?
Not recommended for children under 12 without specialist guidance. Adolescents should be supervised by a qualified dermatologist.

25. Does monobenzone cause cancer?
There is no established evidence that monobenzone causes cancer. However, depigmented skin requires lifelong UV protection to prevent sun damage.

26. What happens if monobenzone gets on healthy skin?
Wash off immediately. Monobenzone will depigment any skin it contacts regularly. Always wash hands within 2 minutes of application.

27. Can I get a reaction to monobenzone?
Yes. Contact dermatitis occurs in 5-10% of patients. Stop use and consult a dermatologist if you experience a widespread severe reaction.

28. How do I manage skin irritation from monobenzone?
Reduce frequency to once daily, apply a thin layer only, use a fragrance-free moisturizer on surrounding skin, and consult your dermatologist if irritation is severe.

29. Is sun protection really necessary?
Absolutely. SPF 50+ daily is mandatory — not optional — from day one of treatment and for life afterwards. Depigmented skin has no natural UV protection.

30. Can I swim or exercise while using monobenzone?
Yes, but apply sunscreen before outdoor swimming or exercise, reapply after water exposure, and avoid peak sun hours. Sweat does not significantly affect the cream's efficacy.

Storage and Product Questions

31. How should I store monobenzone cream?
Store below 25°C in a cool, dry, air-conditioned room away from direct sunlight. Do not refrigerate. Keep out of reach of children.

32. Does heat affect monobenzone cream?
Yes. Sustained temperatures above 25°C can degrade the active ingredient. In GCC countries, always store in air-conditioned spaces, especially during summer months.

33. How long does monobenzone cream last once opened?
Use within 12 months of opening. The product shelf life is 2 years from manufacture. Always check the expiry date on packaging.

35. How much monobenzone do I need per month?
