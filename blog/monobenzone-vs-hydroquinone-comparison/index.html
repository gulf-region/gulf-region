The Key Difference in One Sentence

Monobenzone permanently destroys melanocytes. Hydroquinone temporarily suppresses melanin production. This single difference determines everything about which agent is right for your condition.

How Monobenzone Works

Monobenzone (MBEH) enters melanocytes and is oxidized by tyrosinase, generating quinone free radicals that permanently destroy the pigment-producing cell. This is an irreversible cellular destruction — the melanocytes cannot regenerate once eliminated.

This mechanism makes monobenzone far more potent than hydroquinone, and is why it is only appropriate for patients with extensive vitiligo who have decided on permanent depigmentation.

How Hydroquinone Works

Hydroquinone inhibits tyrosinase — the enzyme responsible for the first step in melanin biosynthesis — reducing the amount of melanin produced. It does not destroy melanocytes; it suppresses them. When treatment stops, melanocyte activity resumes and pigmentation gradually returns.

This reversibility makes hydroquinone appropriate for conditions where ongoing treatment is manageable, such as melasma or post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation.

When to Choose Monobenzone

Monobenzone is the appropriate choice when:

  • You have vitiligo covering 50% or more of body surface area
  • Repigmentation therapy (steroids, NB-UVB, PUVA) has failed after 12+ months
  • You have made an informed, permanent decision to depigment remaining skin
  • You are under qualified dermatologist supervision
⚠️ Monobenzone is NOT appropriate for cosmetic skin lightening, melasma, or age spots. Its permanent effect makes it unsuitable for conditions that hydroquinone can effectively address.

When to Choose Hydroquinone

Hydroquinone is the appropriate choice for:

  • Melasma: Dark patches caused by hormonal changes (pregnancy, contraceptives)
  • Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation: Dark marks after acne, eczema or injury
  • Age spots: Solar lentigines on face and hands
  • Uneven skin tone: General hyperpigmentation in non-vitiligo patients
  • Any patient who prefers a reversible option

Can You Use Both Together?

Some dermatologists use combination protocols in specific clinical scenarios, sometimes as part of a modified Kligman formula (hydroquinone + tretinoin + steroid). However, combining monobenzone and hydroquinone significantly increases potency and side effect risk. Never combine without explicit dermatologist supervision.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between monobenzone and hydroquinone?
Monobenzone permanently destroys melanocytes (permanent depigmentation). Hydroquinone temporarily suppresses melanin production (reversible). This makes them suitable for completely different conditions.
Can hydroquinone be used for vitiligo?
Hydroquinone can help with limited vitiligo-adjacent hyperpigmentation but is not FDA-approved for extensive vitiligo depigmentation. Monobenzone is the FDA-approved standard for permanent vitiligo depigmentation.
Which is stronger, monobenzone or hydroquinone?
Monobenzone is significantly more potent. While hydroquinone suppresses melanin temporarily, monobenzone permanently eliminates melanocytes. This greater potency is why monobenzone is reserved for extensive vitiligo only.
Can I use monobenzone for melasma?
No. Monobenzone is not appropriate for melasma. Its permanent, irreversible effect would eliminate pigment cells entirely — far beyond what is needed for melasma treatment. Hydroquinone 4-8% under dermatologist supervision is the standard for melasma.
How long do hydroquinone results last?
Hydroquinone results last as long as treatment continues. When stopped, melanocyte activity resumes and pigmentation gradually returns, typically within 3-6 months.
